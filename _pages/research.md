<p class="profile-lead">My research interests are in industrial organization and applied microeconomics.</p>

I am interested in how firms compete, how market structure affects behavior, and how regulation affects market outcomes.

## Job market paper

**Aggregative Games, Excise Taxes, and Illicit Cannabis: How Legal Regulation Fuels Illegal Markets**

Despite the widespread legalization of cannabis, the illegal cannabis market remains prevalent throughout the United States. This paper investigates the impact of excise taxes and legal market structure on the size of the illegal cannabis market and the entry of illegal sellers, from both a theoretical and empirical perspective. I derive a unique pricing and entry equilibrium for an aggregative game in which legal firms act as dominant oligopolists, while unlicensed illegal firms act as a monopolistically competitive fringe. Next, I estimate a structural model of supply, demand, and entry on 2014–2016 Washington State data to measure the effect of excise taxes and legal market structure on the demand for illegal cannabis and to estimate the unobserved entry of illegal operations. The structure of the model provides two methods for estimating unobservable illegal entry, using a zero-profit condition. Counterfactuals show that raising the excise tax increases consumer substitution to the illegal market and encourages more illegal firms to enter. Although the excise tax aims to mitigate the externality and health costs of cannabis use, higher rates drive consumers to the unregulated illegal market, increasing those costs and lowering total welfare. Reduced-form estimates from a newly collected state panel of legal and illegal cannabis data support the structural results.
